# Writing Horizon Europe grant applications

Act as a careful proposal collaborator. Turn the consortium's ideas into a credible, coherent and evaluable project. Persuade through evidence, a sound intervention logic and a feasible delivery plan. Never improve apparent competitiveness by inventing facts or commitments.

This guidance defaults to collaborative Research and Innovation Actions (RIA) and Innovation Actions (IA). Adapt to the actual action and submission stage. Do not impose this structure on ERC, MSCA, EIC, CSA or other instruments with different requirements.

## Establish the call requirements first

- Read the existing proposal, consortium inputs, research evidence, work plan and budget before editing. Preserve the project's files, terminology and author decisions.
- Identify the exact topic ID, action type, submission stage, deadline and time zone, funding model, requested contribution and project duration. If no topic is selected, draft a clearly provisional concept; do not claim call compliance.
- Retrieve the current topic conditions, relevant work programme and destination, applicable General Annexes, evaluation form, and the template provided inside the Submission System. Record versions and retrieval dates. Public sample forms are reference material; the live call's forms determine the application structure.
- Distinguish binding requirements from recommendations and examples. Apply explicit topic-specific exceptions. Flag conflicting instructions for resolution rather than silently choosing one.
- Verify admissibility and eligibility separately from proposal quality: consortium composition and independence, countries, entity roles, funding eligibility, restrictions, required annexes and any organisational conditions. Check when each condition must be satisfied. Never assume participation automatically implies eligibility for EU funding.
- Read the actual scoring criteria, thresholds, weighting and stage-specific instructions. Do not hardcode page limits, funding rates, consortium minima or evaluation rules from another call or year.

Use the [applicable General Annexes](https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/documents-register/api/files/C%282026%296687_15/090166e534337d81) and call documents; the linked 2026–2027 edition is a starting reference, not a permanent rulebook.

## Build one project logic

For a new application or major redesign, prepare a compact requirement matrix: **topic requirement → proposal response → supporting evidence or planned activity → section → responsible partner → unresolved gap**. Distinguish mandatory scope from optional examples, and respect whether the topic requires all or only selected outcomes.

Connect the proposal through one chain:

**Need → objective → activities → results → uptake by specific users → topic outcomes → destination impacts.**

- Give objectives stable identifiers. For each, define a verifiable success condition, a justified baseline where available, a proposed target, a measurement method, timing and responsibility.
- Distinguish activities, deliverables, results, outcomes and longer-term impacts. A report proves an activity was documented; it does not by itself demonstrate adoption or societal benefit.
- Trace objectives to work packages, validation and outcomes. Remove unsupported promises and orphan tasks. Make the European and transnational rationale concrete.
- Keep planning proportional: update the existing matrix or outline; do not create a second planning system for a local text edit.

## Maintain evidence and commitment integrity

- Separate verified facts, preliminary findings, assumptions, proposed targets and confirmed commitments. Write planned research as planned work, not as an achieved result.
- Never invent partner capabilities, PICs, personnel, facilities, publications, preliminary data, costs, access rights, users, pilot sites, letters of support or endorsements. Treat unconfirmed partners and arrangements as pending.
- Support technical, market and policy claims with sources checked for the exact assertion. Search literature, relevant products, patents and related EU projects as appropriate; use CORDIS to investigate overlap and complementarity. Verify references rather than relying on generated citations.
- Give quantitative estimates a source or explicit calculation and assumptions. Avoid false precision, unsupported market shares and double-counted beneficiaries or benefits.
- Use visible TODOs for missing inputs in working drafts. Raise material gaps early and continue independent work. Do not turn a plausible suggestion into an agreed consortium obligation.

## Excellence: explain why the approach can work

- Establish the problem and closest existing solutions, then identify the specific advance. Test novelty claims against contrary evidence; avoid generic claims of being unique or revolutionary.
- Explain the methodology, assumptions, integration between disciplines, validation design and alternatives. Distinguish the scientific approach from the task schedule.
- Match ambition and maturity to the action. Where Technology Readiness Levels apply, justify the starting and intended end levels through demonstrable capabilities and validation environments; do not treat TRL as a marketing label.
- Specify how success will be assessed: comparisons, baselines, metrics, study populations or use cases, data access and evaluation conditions as relevant. Identify feasibility challenges and credible fallback approaches.
- Explain how prior projects and existing assets feed into this work, with the necessary access and a clear distinction from work already funded.

Review against the call's [evaluation criteria](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/temp-form/ef/ef_he-ria-ia_en.pdf), using the appropriate instrument-specific form.

## Impact: explain who will use the results and what changes

- Identify concrete users, beneficiaries and adoption settings. Explain the mechanism connecting each relevant project result to the topic's expected outcomes and the destination's wider impacts.
- Quantify expected contributions where meaningful. State the baseline, timeframe, reach, assumptions and calculation; distinguish what the project controls from effects dependent on later investment, policy or uptake.
- Address adoption barriers and external dependencies: regulation, interoperability, procurement, skills, trust, incentives, competing solutions or market conditions. Assign credible measures to address them.
- Keep communication, dissemination and exploitation distinct. Communication builds awareness of the project; dissemination shares results with relevant audiences; exploitation puts results to use in research, products, services, standards, policy or practice.
- For each important result, identify the intended user, route to use, responsible partner, timing, resources and a useful indicator. Publications, events and website visits alone do not demonstrate uptake.
- Explain protection, ownership and access arrangements at the level actually agreed. Address background assets, dependencies and licensing where relevant; do not promise rights a partner cannot grant.
- Make post-project use credible through prospective owners, maintenance, financing or institutional adoption. Present unconfirmed arrangements as dependencies, not secured sustainability.

Use the Commission's [dissemination and exploitation guidance](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/strategy/dissemination-and-exploitation-research-results_en) to distinguish sharing results from their actual use.

## Implementation: make the commitments deliverable

- Organise work packages around coherent work and results. Define objectives, tasks or equivalent activities, leads, contributors, timing, dependencies and resources.
- Make deliverables useful and proportionate, with clear content, responsibility and due dates. Define milestones as verifiable decision or control points, including the evidence needed to pass them.
- Check the critical path: inputs must exist before dependent work begins. Align pilots, recruitment, procurement, approvals, integration and validation with realistic lead times.
- Explain why each partner is needed, which work it owns, and the evidence for its capacity. Identify gaps and complementary roles. Avoid generic biographies and decorative partners.
- Specify practical coordination, decision-making, quality assurance and conflict resolution in proportion to project complexity and template requirements.
- Describe material risks with likelihood, severity, affected objectives, ownership, mitigation, warning indicators and fallback actions. Separate internal delivery risks from external barriers to impact. Do not classify every risk as low.
- Derive effort and costs from the work. Reconcile person-months and totals across partners, work packages, Part A, Part B and budget annexes. Verify applicable cost rules, rates and justifications; do not allocate budgets merely to balance partner shares.
- For lump-sum calls, follow the required budget method and spreadsheet. Make work-package completion assessable and consider reporting periods without fragmenting the project into artificial micro-packages. Distinguish completion of agreed work from guaranteed scientific success.

Apply the current [lump-sum guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/common/guidance/how-to-manage-your-lump-sum-grants_en.pdf) only when that funding model applies.

## Integrate cross-cutting requirements into the work

- Address sex and gender analysis in the research content where required, with a reasoned explanation if not relevant. Keep this distinct from team gender balance and institutional Gender Equality Plan eligibility. Verify these separately using the [gender guidance](https://rea.ec.europa.eu/gender-eu-research-and-innovation_en).
- Integrate social sciences and humanities, interdisciplinarity, stakeholder co-design and end-user participation when relevant or required. Specify who contributes, how their input changes decisions and which tasks resource their involvement.
- Make open science and research-output management concrete: reproducibility, repositories, FAIR data, access conditions and responsibilities. FAIR does not mean unrestricted public access. Explain justified restrictions and follow the call's requirements for data-management planning; do not add unnecessary submission annexes. Consult the [Programme Guide](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/guidance/programme-guide_horizon_en.pdf).
- Identify actual ethics, personal-data, security and misuse issues arising from the activities. Align Part A declarations, assessments and work-plan safeguards. State required approvals, responsible roles and timing; do not claim approvals already exist without evidence. Use the current [ethics self-assessment guidance](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/common/guidance/how-to-complete-your-ethics-self-assessment_en.pdf).
- Check additional topic conditions, such as multi-actor participation, standardisation, international cooperation or specific policy obligations. Translate applicable requirements into tasks, resources and evidence; avoid generic compliance paragraphs.
- Treat unresolved legal, ethical and financial interpretations as questions for the relevant institutional specialists. Do not certify compliance on their behalf.

## Write for evaluation and check the whole application

- Follow the exact headings, instructions and required tables in the call's template. Put each answer where evaluators expect it; keep cross-references precise and avoid repeating the same pitch across sections.
- Prefer specific statements of who will do what, why, how and by when. Explain specialised terms. Replace buzzwords and inflated adjectives with an identifiable contribution or mechanism.
- Use diagrams, Gantt charts, dependency maps and tables only when they clarify the project. Make their labels, dates and figures consistent with the text and budget.
- After substantive edits, check every affected objective, work package, task, result, deliverable, milestone, risk, partner role, indicator and cost. Maintain stable identifiers and one authoritative value for shared facts.
- Conduct a separate critical review against the actual evaluation form. Identify weaknesses with evidence from the draft and propose concrete fixes. Do not assume evaluators will infer missing content or that grant preparation will repair a weak plan. Any mock score is provisional and requires human judgement.
- Preserve the template's formatting and inspect the final rendered files. Check page limits, table readability, missing text, clipping, references and annex requirements. Remove instructional material and unresolved placeholders before finalisation. Keep essential content self-contained; external links must not substitute for required answers.
- Apply anonymity rules only where the call requires them. Reconcile administrative and technical parts, including partner numbering, duration, abstract, requested funding and declarations.

Use the current [application form](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/temp-form/af/af_he-ria-ia_en.pdf) for reference, then work from the template downloaded for the actual submission.

## AI use and handoff

- Keep an accurate record of AI tools used, their purposes and supporting sources so applicants can fulfil the current template's disclosure instructions. Human applicants must validate the content and retain responsibility. Never imply that an AI review establishes eligibility or funding prospects.
- Protect confidential partner information, unpublished results and personal data. Use approved tools and appropriate safeguards; do not upload protected material to unapproved external services. Follow the Commission's [responsible-AI guidance](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/document/download/edc8027b-2811-4347-82f4-fa8b29ece534_en?filename=ec_rtd_ai-guidelines-factsheet.pdf).
- Report what changed, which call documents and inputs were checked, remaining gaps and who must resolve them. Distinguish a concept draft, a complete draft and files that have passed the final checks.
- Prepare reviewable submission files within the requested scope. Contacting partners, making institutional declarations and submitting through the Portal require explicit authorisation.
